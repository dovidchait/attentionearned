## Google Drive Bulk Downloader (Windows)

Clients send footage in Google Drive folders. Downloading a big folder from the browser means Google zips it into 2 GB chunks, half of them fail, and you start over. This tool pulls the whole folder straight to your drive instead.

- **Paste a folder link, pick a destination, click Start.** Subfolders included.
- **Only grabs what you don't have yet.** It compares Drive against your local folder by filename, so you can re-run it as a client keeps uploading.
- **Filter by type.** Type `*.mp4` to skip the proxies, PDFs and stills.
- **Resumes.** Stop it or lose your connection; run it again and finished files are skipped.
- **No setup.** No Google Cloud project or API keys. The first run asks you to sign in to Google once in your browser.

[Download GDriveDownloader.exe &#8594;](/downloads/GDriveDownloader.exe)

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.

### Before you run it

**Windows will warn you.** The exe isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC". Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. If you'd rather not, the [Python source](/downloads/gdrive_downloader.py) is one readable file; run it with `python gdrive_downloader.py`. The [SHA-256 checksum](/downloads/GDriveDownloader.exe.sha256) is published alongside.

**What it accesses.** It asks Google for read-only access to your Drive, so it can't change or delete anything there. Your sign-in is saved only on your PC, in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\GDriveDownloader`. Nothing goes through our servers. Delete that folder to sign out completely.

**How it works.** It's a front end for [rclone](https://rclone.org), the open-source sync tool, which it downloads from rclone.org on first run.

**Google Photos links don't work.** Google blocks outside apps from downloading Photos albums. Use **Download all** in the album's ⋮ menu, or ask the sender to share a Drive folder instead.

**Limits.** It uses rclone's shared Google connection, so Google may throttle very large jobs (hundreds of GB in a day). If you hit that, wait and run it again; it picks up where it stopped.
