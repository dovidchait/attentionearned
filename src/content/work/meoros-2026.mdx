This year they came back with a $400,000 goal and a different problem.

Mrs. Sara Gittelson, board member and co-founder of Meoros, put it in one line: "Let's face it, when is the last time you watched a school video that wasn't from your kid's school?"

People already know why Meoros exists. Last year's video took care of that. A second "why we exist" video would get a nod from the parents and nothing from anyone else.

The goal this year wasn't awareness. It was action — funders, ambassadors, parents and students actually raising. So the question changed. How do you get a random person from a random place to care about your kid's school?

The answer wasn't a story. It was a feeling: make fundraising fun, and make something people actually want to watch and share.

So we handed the girls the cameras and the questions. They interviewed their teachers, their parents and each other. "Why do you get up every morning to come and teach at Meoros?" "Who is your favorite student?" (No answer.)

They got paid in sugar. We kept the bloopers. We kept the ending, where nobody is sure the camera is still rolling.

Then we built the rollout around how people actually share. A blooper teaser the day before launch. A short case study on last year vs this year. WhatsApp status cuts sized to post in one tap. Subtitles on everything, for everyone watching on mute.

A second campaign isn't a sequel. When the goal changes, the video should too.
